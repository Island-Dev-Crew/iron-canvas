# Phase 3.9: PACKAGE — Design PRD + THE SCORE ★v6

> **Origin:** Inspired by the Antigravity video workflow (Prompt 2 pattern).
> The Orchestrator converts all research into one unified Design PRD — the single
> document consumed by all parallel Build Agents simultaneously.
>
> A weak PRD = conflicting agent outputs that don't integrate.
> A precise PRD = coherent, unified product that passes verification first try.
>
> This is the most important document in the entire pipeline.
>
> **★v6 — PACKAGE writes four things.** The PRD; **the score** (`score.json` — the motion
> contract: one motion sentence, one signature, one clock); the **engine jobs** — after the
> **Artifact Assessment Gate**, which moved here from Phase 5 so the ASSET FORGE lane (5a) can run
> in parallel with the build agents; and the **design contract** (`design-contract.json` — the
> design plan made machine-readable). `node scripts/ic-preflight.mjs` must pass before anything is
> dispatched; `node scripts/ic-contract.mjs` checks every build from the first one onward.

## The Rule

The Design PRD is the Orchestrator's primary output before agent dispatch. It contains everything a Build Agent needs to execute their domain independently — no guessing, no assumptions, no conflicts with other agents.

★v6: motion is authored, not sprinkled. Before a line of motion code, every section gets an
emotion, a job and a role; the page gets one motion sentence, exactly one signature and one clock.
*The agent writes the score; the runtime performs it.*

---

## How to Produce the Design PRD

The Orchestrator reads all Phase 1-3.5 outputs and synthesizes them into the PRD template below. Every section maps directly to a Build Agent's domain. ★v6 order of work: PRD Sections 1–3 → the score (3.9a) → PRD Section 4 from the score → the Assessment Gate + engine jobs (3.9b) → PRD Sections 5–11 → preflight (3.9c) → the design contract (3.9d).

## 3.9a ★v6 — Write THE SCORE (the motion contract)

> **The agent writes the score. The runtime performs it.** Motion coordinated through prose breaks
> in predictable ways: two things own the scroll, two atmospheres fight, reduced motion leaks, the
> climax lands next to another climax, and nobody can say why a section moves. The score replaces
> prose with a contract — `iron_canvas_output/score.json` (schema: `runtime/score.schema.json`),
> performed at FORGE by `runtime/canvas-score.js`, preflighted here (3.9c), proven at VERIFY
> (Axis 7). Full doctrine: [references/cinematic-score.md](../references/cinematic-score.md).

**1. The motion sentence — the register sets its length** (a Studio bible's arc is the starting point):

```
R0  establish → resolve
R1  establish → reveal → resolve
R2  establish → accelerate → pause → reveal → resolve
R3  establish → accelerate → pause → reveal → recover → climax → resolve
R4  the full sentence, as a camera rail through one world
```

Two loud beats never touch: at R2+ a `pause` or `recover` sits between any two high-energy acts.
The quiet chapter is not filler — it is the reason the climax lands.

**2. One act per section.** `id` · `el` · `stage` (its beat) · `job` (orient · establish_stakes ·
explain_system · compare · trace_evidence · show_progression · explore_network · inspect_detail ·
commit · handoff) · `role` (`signature` — exactly one per page · `support` · `stillness`) ·
`intent` · `emotion { in, out }` in words a person would use · `trigger` (`load` · `enter` ·
`scrub`) · `personality` · `pin / length / scrub / hold` for scrub acts · `reduced` (what the
settled frame means) · `fallback` · `shots[]`. Start from the mechanism sentence: the signature act
acts it out — if you cannot say what it shows in one sentence, the score is not ready.

**3. Shots — verbs on a timeline.** One vocabulary of 17 verbs — enter `fade · rise · unveil ·
bloom · focus · tilt · wipe · iris` · exit `sink · lift · defocus · dolly` · scrub `drift` ·
special `draw · count · scramble · sweep` — or a hook (`call`, e.g. the WebGL peak). Seven
personalities: `silk · tide · gravity · spark · snap · bloom · drift`. Two to four shots per act,
one in the pause; `sweep` and `bloom` once per page; `count` only on numbers that are true; a
`note` (the director's note the critic reads) on any shot whose reason is not obvious.

**4. Timing follows emotional weight.** *"More frames at critical moments (lid opening, ingredient
explosion). Fewer frames during slow transitions (subtle glow, settling)."* The signature act gets
the most scroll — 200–280 % of the viewport, pinned, with `hold` 0.3–0.5 so the final frame, where
the meaning sits, rests; quiet acts get 100–140 %, unpinned, one shot; inside an act, shots crowd
around the peak.

**5. The contract's teeth** (checked by the preflight, 3.9c):

```
ONE SIGNATURE      exactly one act has role: "signature"                                   (error)
ONE CLOCK          scroll-owned motion only in scrub acts; WebGL subscribes to progress     (error)
ONE VOCABULARY     one house personality + at most one accent; only the 17 verbs            (error · warning)
QUIET BETWEEN LOUD at R2+, no two high-energy acts adjacent without a pause or recover      (warning)
EVERY ACT FEELS    intent + emotion.in / emotion.out filled — never "TODO", never "Static"  (error)
EVERY ACT RESTS    reduced says what the settled frame communicates                         (warning)
MOBILE             no pinning on the web pack at R0–R3 — declare a mobile transformation    (warning)
SCRUB CONSISTENCY  a scrub act has a length or an end; an enter act has no length           (error)
```

Budgets the score must fit (web pack, R3/R4 defaults): first-load JS ≤ 180 kB gzipped with
three.js lazy-loaded · above-the-fold media ≤ 6 MB · one display face + one text face · DPR 1.5
desktop / 1 mobile · particle counts set by viewport. One dominant spectacle per viewport; aurora,
iridescence, cloud gradients and frosted glass are *alternative* atmospheres — pick one.

One act, for shape (a complete score: references/cinematic-score.md §5):

```json
{ "id": "reveal", "el": "#act-reveal", "stage": "reveal", "job": "explain_system", "role": "signature",
  "intent": "the first sight of the mechanism", "emotion": { "in": "curious", "out": "certain" },
  "trigger": "scrub", "pin": true, "length": "240%", "scrub": 0.9, "hold": 0.4, "personality": "bloom",
  "reduced": "the mechanism diagram, fully drawn", "fallback": "the diagram as a poster frame",
```

## 3.9b ★v6 — The Artifact Assessment Gate + Engine Jobs (moved here from Phase 5)

The gate now runs at the end of PACKAGE, so every artifact — and every engine job — is known before
FORGE starts, and the ASSET FORGE lane (5a) runs in parallel with the build agents instead of after
them. Phase 5 GENERATE then judges what was forged (the media tournament). Every site gets scored —
this is not optional (ROUTING.md § ARTIFACT ASSESSMENT GATE).

**A site with no images that completes Iron Canvas with still no images is a FAILED run** (R2+;
code-driven and generative visuals count).

Score each criterion 1-5:

```
CRITERION 1: IMAGE ABSENCE (1-5)
  5 = No images anywhere on the site (ZERO imagery — like Crown Collection)
  3 = Some images but weak, generic, or heavily missing in key sections
  1 = Rich, high-quality existing imagery throughout every section
  Score: ___

CRITERION 2: VISUAL STORYTELLING OPPORTUNITY (1-5)
  5 = Physical product that MUST be seen to be desired (body butter, car, food)
  3 = Service/concept that benefits from visual representation
  1 = Pure information/text service with no visual anchor
  Score: ___

CRITERION 3: SCROLL SEQUENCE VIABILITY (1-5)
  5 = Product has a reveal story (open/close, ingredient explosion, transformation)
  3 = A process or journey could be shown through scrolled frames
  1 = No natural reveal — content is better delivered statically
  Score: ___

CRITERION 4: COMPETITIVE PREMIUM GAP (1-5)
  5 = Competitors have cinematic imagery and the site looks cheap without it
  3 = Would help differentiate but not critical for credibility
  1 = Industry norm is text-forward — imagery would feel out of place
  Score: ___

CRITERION 5: BRAND PERSONALITY ALIGNMENT (1-5)
  5 = Bold ≥ 7, Avant-garde ≥ 6, feel profile is "luxury/cinematic/premium"
  3 = Mixed scores — some visuals appropriate
  1 = Serious + Corporate + Minimal — artifacts would feel intrusive
  Score: ___

TOTAL: ___ / 25
```

  (`clay.mp4` + `camera-rail.json`) · `hero-object` (a seeded Draco GLB, budgeted — Tier II ≤ 150k
  tris / 1.5 MB, Tier III ≤ 600k / 8 MB; over budget fails the job) · `turntable` · `matcap`.
- **Seedance 2.5** (`node engines/video/video.mjs plan|run|ingest --job <shot>.json`): the shot's
  TIMELINE comes from the score's beats (`[0–3 s] establish — …`, `[3–6 s] reveal — …`); one camera
  move per beat, cause before effect, one material and one light; loops 4–8 s with first frame =
  last frame. `plan` prints the cost before anything is spent (≈ $0.47/s at 720p).
- **Twin camera** (Blender on + video on + depth ≥ 0.4 + register ≥ R2; expected at R4): Blender
  renders the signature's camera move as flat grey clay → Seedance takes it as `@Video1` (camera
  path, speed and blocking only) with a style frame as `@Image1` (palette, material and light only)
  → the site's WebGL rail reads the same `camera-rail.json`. The film and the site match shot for shot.
- **Audio** (`node engines/audio/audio.mjs plan|run --job <cue>.json`): cues at the signature and
  the commit; beds only at R3+; in the page, off by default behind a visible toggle.
- A Studio bible's `jobs` are the starting jobs — rewrite their placeholder stations and timelines
  from the final score. Names follow the Studio: `<id>-rail.clay.json`, `<id>-hero.object.json`,
  `<id>-film.shot.json` / `<id>-loop.shot.json`, `<id>-commit.cue.json`, `<id>-bed.cue.json`.
- Every refusal goes on the record in `engine_gates`; every missing engine takes its fallback:
  Blender → procedural three.js → §19 point cloud → Tier I still · video → HyperFrames → §19 flow
  field → poster · audio → WebAudio synth → no audio UI.

## 3.9d ★v6 — The Design Contract (taste enforced, not advisory)

The design plan (CD3 Law 3 — PRD Section 1) made machine-readable: `design-contract.json`, started
from `templates/design-contract.json`. It records **4–6 named colours with roles** (plus allowed
shades, each with a reason), the **type roles** with a named fallback floor each, the **motion
budget** (which curves, which durations — from the role tokens), the **radius, shadow and border
policies**, and the **hard fails**.

Template defaults are **decision slots, never pre-filled choices**: a blurred shadow ladder,
translucent hairlines, a radius ladder or four easing curves appear only when the DNA chose them
(shipping template pre-fills as if chosen is Anti-Pattern #21 DEFAULTS AS DECISIONS).

```bash
node scripts/ic-contract.mjs --contract design-contract.json <build dir>
# exit 0 clean · 1 contract violations · 2 usage error — every departure reported with file:line
```

From the first build onward the lint reads every stylesheet (`.css`, `<style>` blocks, `style=""`)
and reports colour literals outside the palette, unknown faces, curves and durations outside the
budget, GSAP ease names in CSS, radius outside the policy, blurred shadows under a hard policy,
translucent borders when forbidden, and the contract's own forbidden patterns. A contract that
holds only where someone happens to notice is advisory; this one runs in CI. It cannot judge feel —
a critic still does. Exemplar: `showcase/origin.contract.json`.
